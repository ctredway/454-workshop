Every part of 454 Control's screen, and what each control does. For your first job from start to finish,
see the [Control quick start](control-quickstart.html). The ⓘ beside each section in the app gives a short
reminder of the same.

:::caution
454 Control moves a real machine. Keep a hand near the stop, wear eye and ear protection, and run anything
new in the air first.
:::

## The screen

<Shot src={overview} alt="454 Control connected to a machine, with a job loaded: the 3D preview of the toolpaths on the left, the Machine tab on the right, and the position and playback bar along the bottom." caption="Connected, with the sample job loaded and its preview partway through." />

- **The header** shows the loaded file's name and, once connected, a red **STOP** button, the machine's
  state (such as *Idle*, *Run* or *Alarm*), whether the spindle is running, and **Hold** and **Resume**. On
  the right: **Docs**, **454 Design**, **Settings**, the sample file, and **Open** for a G-code file (`.nc`,
  `.gcode`, `.tap` or `.ngc`). You can also drop a file anywhere on the window.
- **The 3D preview** draws the job: cuts as solid lines, rapids dashed. Left-drag to orbit, right-drag to
  pan, scroll to zoom. The cube in the corner turns the view to Top, Front, Left and so on, and **Fit**
  frames the whole job.
- **The summary** above the tabs gives the job's extents in X, Y and Z, its estimated run time, how far it
  cuts and travels, and its tools.
- **The tabs:** [Machine](#the-machine-tab), [Code](#the-code-tab), [Toolpaths](#the-toolpaths-tab) and
  [Checks](#the-checks-tab).
- **The bar along the bottom:** the [position readout and playback](#the-position-and-playback-bar).

## The Machine tab

Everything for running the machine, in the order you use it: connecting and homing, setting up (jogging and
zeroing, probing, the spindle), running a job, and quick actions. The console opens in a window of its own. It's
the tab Control opens on.

<Shot src={machineTab} alt="The Machine tab, connected: Disconnect; Home, Unlock and Reset; Set up, with Jog & zero, Probe Z and Spindle; Run loaded file, Recover, Hold, Resume and End job; quick actions; and the Console button with the controller's latest message." />

### Connecting

<Steps>

1. Switch the machine on and plug it in by USB.
2. Press **Connect to machine** and pick your controller from the browser's list. After the first time, the
   button says **Reconnect** and skips the list; **Choose port…** picks a different one.
3. Control reads the controller's settings and asks to **home** the machine. Say yes: homing is required
   before a job, because lifting clear, parking and the BitSetter all use the machine's own coordinates.

</Steps>

Control talks to GRBL 1.1 controllers over USB, which covers the Shapeoko and Nomad. The web version needs
**Chrome or Edge on a computer**, because other browsers can't talk to USB devices; the desktop app works on
its own. Everything else, including opening and checking G-code, works anywhere.

### Machine

- **Home ($H)** runs the homing cycle, finding each axis's limit switch.
- **Unlock ($X)** clears an alarm without homing. Use it with care: until you home, the machine doesn't
  know where it is.
- **Reset** stops everything at once, as the controller's reset does.
- The line underneath shows the **machine position**, in the machine's own coordinates.

### Jog and zero

**Jog & zero**, under **Set up**, moves the machine by hand and sets your work zero: where the job's X0, Y0 and Z0 are on
the material.

<Shot src={jogPanel} alt="The jog panel: a STOP button, the work position in large figures, step sizes 0.025, 0.1, 1, 10 and Fast, buttons for each direction in X, Y and Z, rapid-position buttons for nine points around the table, and Zero X, Zero Y, Zero Z, Zero all, Go to XY zero, Z +6 mm, Probe and Change tool." />

- **Move** sets how far each press goes: 0.025, 0.1, 1 or 10 mm. In **Fast**, the machine moves while you
  hold a button or key, at the **Fast XY** and **Fast Z** speeds, and stops the moment you let go.
- **The arrow buttons** move X and Y, including diagonally; **Z+** and **Z−** move Z. On the keyboard, while
  the panel is open: <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> for X, <kbd>↑</kbd> <kbd>↓</kbd> for Y, <kbd>PgUp</kbd> <kbd>PgDn</kbd> for Z.
- **Rapid position** moves quickly to one of nine points around the table: the corners, the middles of the
  edges, and the centre. It needs a homed machine.
- **Work zero:** **Zero X**, **Zero Y**, **Zero Z** or **Zero all** makes the current position zero on those
  axes. The zero is stored in the controller, so it survives homing and restarting Control.
- **Go to XY zero** raises Z first, then moves over the job's X0 Y0. **Z +6 mm** lifts the tool 6 mm from
  where it is, as in Carbide Motion. **Probe…** opens the BitZero panel.
- **Change tool…** changes the bit outside a job, as Carbide Motion's Change tool does. The machine lifts Z right
  up, stops the spindle, goes to the tool-change position (Settings → Machine → Parking) and waits. Change the bit,
  then press **Measure the new bit**: with the BitSetter set up, the new bit is measured and its length applied, so
  your Z zero is kept. If the bit that set Z zero wasn't measured then (you answered **Later**, or reconnected
  since), it's measured first, before the machine goes to the tool-change position. Without a BitSetter, set Z zero again for the
  new bit before cutting; until you do, Start warns you. It needs a homed machine, and isn't offered during a
  job, whose tool changes have their own prompt.

### Probe

**Probe Z…**, under **Set up**, sets Z zero with the BitZero touch plate, which puts Z0 at the top of your material.

<Steps>

1. Set the plate's thickness in **Settings → Accessories**, measured with calipers from its bottom face to
   the top face the tool touches, to a tenth of a millimetre. If the number is too small, Z zero lands that
   much above the material and every cut is that much shallow; too large, and every cut is that much deep.
2. Put the whole plate flat on top of the material, not hooked over a corner, close to where you'll cut,
   and jog the tool over it, 2 to 10 mm above.
3. Touch the pin to the plate to check the circuit, then probe. Control finds the plate quickly, backs off,
   and measures again slowly for accuracy.
4. Check the result with a piece of paper under the tool before cutting.

</Steps>

### Spindle

**Spindle…**, under **Set up**, opens a small window: type a speed and press **Start (M3)**, or **Spindle
off**. Starting needs the machine idle, and the spindle chip in the header stops the spindle from anywhere.
With a VFD, the speed shown is what was asked for, not a measurement. Starting by hand here doesn't wait for
the spindle to reach speed, so give it a few seconds before cutting. Jobs are different: Control adds a spin-up wait after every spindle start
automatically, set in **Settings → Accessories**.

### Running a job

<Steps>

1. Load the file, check its preview and the **Checks** tab, and set your work zero.
2. Press **Run loaded file**. If the job changes tools, Control first asks how each change should work (see
   below).
3. Read the summary of what will happen, then press **Start job**.
4. While it runs, **Hold** pauses, **Resume** continues, and **End job** stops it: it lifts clear before
   stopping the spindle.

</Steps>

<Shot src={startDialog} alt="The Start this job? dialog: the file name, the cut extents, the checks, the tool changes and where they happen, the spin-up waits, where the machine lifts and parks, and a reminder to check the zero, the tool and the stop." caption="Before anything moves, Control says exactly what it will do: extents, tool changes, spin-up waits, lifts and the ending." />

**Tool changes.** For a job with more than one tool, Control needs a way to set each new tool's height. With
a BitSetter set up, it measures each tool automatically. Without one, it asks what you'd like to do:

<Shot src={toolChoice} alt="A dialog: This job changes tools, but the BitSetter isn't set up. Choices: Cancel, Set up the BitSetter, or Re-zero at each change." />

At each change, the machine lifts, moves to the tool-change position, stops the spindle and asks for the
new tool; with **Re-zero at each change**, it then asks you to set Z zero again, with the BitZero or by
jogging.

**Adjusting a running job.** While a job runs, **Adjust** in the bar over the preview opens a row of buttons:

| | What it changes |
| --- | --- |
| **F** | The feed rate, as a percentage of what the file asks for: 10 or 1 at a time, or back to 100. |
| **S** | The spindle speed, the same way (VFD spindles only; a router's speed is on its dial). |
| **rapid** | How fast the machine travels between cuts: 25%, 50% or 100%. |
| **Z** | A nudge up (+, shallower) or down (−, deeper) of the whole job, 0.02 or 0.1 mm at a time, for a cut that is a little too deep or not quite through. It takes effect a few moves later, once the moves already handed to the machine are done. |

Every job starts with all four at normal: 100% and no nudge. What you adjust in one job ends with that job.
To carry it into the next job, tick **Keep for the next job**. The next job then asks before it starts,
listing what it would keep, and **Back to normal** there clears it. Setting Z zero always clears the Z nudge.

**Recover** restarts a stopped job from a line, for example after a broken bit: type the line in **from
line**, or scrub the preview to the spot. Control rebuilds the job's state up to that line, moves back
carefully, and lets you back out at any point.

### Quick actions

Saved G-code for things you do often, like **Raise Z to top** or **Spindle warm-up**. **+ Add** makes a new
one, and the pencil edits one. Lines are sent one at a time, each waiting for the controller to confirm the
last, and any error, alarm or reset stops the action at once. They're saved with your machine profile.

### Console

**Console…** opens the raw conversation with the controller in its own window: everything Control sends, and
everything it says back. You can type commands there for anything the app doesn't cover, once connected.

Beside the button, the Machine tab shows the controller's latest message, in red if it's an error. A red number
on the button counts the errors that arrived since you last opened the console, so one isn't missed while the
window is shut.

:::caution
Commands typed in the console are sent exactly as written, with no checks and no lifting or spindle
handling. Treat it as typing straight into the controller.
:::

## The Code tab

<Shot src={codeTab} alt="The Code tab: the job's G-code with line numbers, and below it Follow playback, a jump to toolpath menu, and the file's line and segment count." />

The job's G-code, line by line. Click a line to move the preview there. With **Follow playback** on, the
list scrolls to keep the current line in view as the preview plays. **Jump to toolpath…** goes to the start
of any toolpath named in the file.

## The Toolpaths tab

<Shot src={toolpathsTab} alt="The Toolpaths tab: two toolpaths, Circle Pocket and Outside Profile, each with its tool, share of the time, deepest cut, cutting levels, feed, plunge, spindle speed, area, distance and lines." />

Each toolpath in the file, with its tool, estimated time and share of the job, deepest cut and cutting
levels, feeds, spindle speed, the area it covers, and its lines. Click one to show just that toolpath in the
preview. If a file doesn't name its toolpaths, they're grouped by tool change instead.

### Running only some toolpaths

To run part of a job again without going back to Design or your CAM program, untick **Run** on the toolpaths
to leave out. The tab then shows how many will run (**2/3**), and **Start** runs only the ticked ones. The
start dialog lists what runs and what's left out.

Before each toolpath that comes after a left-out one, Control puts the machine where the file expects it:

1. It lifts Z to traverse height.
2. If the left-out toolpath loaded a different tool, it stops for a tool change, as a job's own tool changes do
   (with the BitSetter, the tool is measured).
3. It goes to where the toolpath starts, starts the spindle at the file's speed with your spin-up wait, and
   comes down: straight down if that's above the work, or close and then at a slow plunge if it starts in it.

Opening a file ticks every toolpath again. **Recover** keeps left-out toolpaths left out, and won't resume
from a line inside one. A toolpath that begins partway along an arc, or a file that moves in machine
coordinates or changes its work offsets between toolpaths, can't be split this way: Start says so and runs
nothing.

:::caution
A left-out toolpath may be the one that cut a part free, or cleared room for the next. Check the preview,
and air-cut if you're unsure.
:::

## The Checks tab

<Shot src={checksTab} alt="The Checks tab: two notes, one for each tool change, each with the line it's on." />

Anything in the file worth knowing before you cut: **errors** (red) that the machine would refuse or that
would make the job go wrong, **warnings** (orange), and **notes** (blue) like tool changes. Click one to go
to its line. A file with nothing to report says so.

## The position and playback bar

<Shot src={footer} alt="The bottom bar: X 56.600, Y 87.250, Z 32.500, the current line and tool, the playback buttons, a speed menu set to 1×, a timeline slider, and 0:54 of 1:27." />

- **X, Y, Z** show the work position: where the tool is relative to your work zero. While connected, that's
  the machine's live position; otherwise, the preview's.
- **line** and **tool** show where the preview is in the file.
- **The playback buttons** go to the start, step back a line, play or pause, step forward a line, and go to
  the end. The menu sets the speed, from 0.5× to 100×, and the slider scrubs through the job. The time on
  the right is the preview's position in the estimated run time.

Playback only moves the preview, never the machine.

## Settings

The gear in the header. Settings are saved as you change them, in your machine profile.

<Shot src={settings} alt="The Settings window on its Machine tab: the machine it recognised, display options, the work area, job zero, stock thickness, the time estimate's rapid rate, and the controller's settings as read." />

- **Machine:** what to show in the preview; the **work area** (from the controller, or a machine you pick),
  where the job's zero sits on the material, and whether to flag moves outside the work area; the **stock
  thickness**, to flag cuts deeper than the material; the rapid rate used for time estimates; and the
  **controller config**, the controller's own settings as read, with **Check controller settings** to look
  them over for anything that would cause trouble.
- **Accessories:** the **BitSetter** (jog over it and **Capture position**; **Test** touches off once to
  check it, changing nothing), the **BitZero** (v1 or v2, and the plate thickness), and the **spindle**
  (trim router or VFD, and the spin-up wait after each start: about 2.5 s suits a router, 7 s a VFD; the default is 7).
- **Job:** **Start & stop high**, which keeps the bit above clamps and stock whenever it's travelling (leave
  it on): at the start of a job it lifts and travels high to the first cut, and after each tool change too.
  If the file starts with a tool change, the machine goes to the tool change first, and to the first cut
  after it; the traverse height; how far to lift at tool changes and the end; and where to change tools and
  where to park at the end of a job.
- **Profile:** the theme (dark or light) and accent colour, and exporting or importing your machine profile,
  including a Carbide Motion `shapeoko.json`.

### Work area

The size of the machine's cutting area, used to draw the table in the 3D view and to check that a job fits.
**From the controller** fills it in from the controller's own travel (`$130` and `$131`) each time you connect.
Pick a machine from the list, or type a size, and Control keeps that instead, even if the controller reports
something different; the console notes the difference when you connect. Choose **From the controller** again to
go back.

### Job zero

Where the drawing's 0,0 sits on the stock: the front-left corner, or the middle. This only affects the preview
and the check for moves outside the work area. It doesn't move your machine's work zero, which you set with the
BitZero or by zeroing the axes.

### Stock thickness

The thickness of the material, used to flag cuts that go deeper than it, which usually means into the
spoilboard. Set it when Z zero is the top of your material. Leave it at 0 to turn the check off.

### Time estimate

How long a job will take, worked out the way GRBL moves the machine. Once the controller's settings are read,
the estimate uses its own limits: the maximum speed and acceleration of each axis (`$110` to `$112`, `$120` to
`$122`) and junction deviation (`$11`), which sets how fast corners can be taken. Short moves and sharp corners
take longer than their feed rate suggests, and the estimate allows for that.

Before the settings are read, it assumes every move runs at its feed rate and rapids at the rapid rate set
here, which underestimates jobs with lots of short moves. The rapid rate changes the estimate only: the
machine's real rapid speed comes from the controller.

### Controller config

The controller's own `$` settings, read back when you connect: travel limits, homing, speeds and the spindle
maximum. Control only reads them. They're changed in the controller itself, and Carbide Motion's setup wizard
writes them for Shapeoko machines. **Check controller settings** compares the ones Control depends on against
what it needs, and reports anything that looks wrong.

### BitSetter

The fixed button the tool touches so the machine can measure the tool's length. Jog the tool over the button
and press **Capture position**; **Test** touches off once to check it, changing nothing.

When you set Z zero, Control offers to measure that tool straight away. That makes it the reference: at Start
the loaded tool is measured against it, and every new tool at each change, so the zero holds through tool
changes, and a bit swapped before you press Start is caught instead of silently shifting Z. It needs a homed
machine, because the button's position is in the machine's own coordinates.

### BitZero

The touch plate used to set your work zero. Control uses it for Z. Choose v1 or v2 and enter the plate's
thickness, measured with calipers from its bottom face to the top face the tool touches: a BitZero v2 is about
15.5 mm, but measure your own, because every depth you cut is off by whatever this number is off. The probe
sets Z0 at the top of the stock: the height where the tool touched, less the thickness. Touch the pin to the
plate to check the circuit before probing, and check the zero with a piece of paper before cutting.

### Game controller

An Xbox-style game controller, USB or Bluetooth, makes a handy pendant at the machine. Tick **Jog with a game
controller** in **Settings → Accessories**, plug the controller in (or pair it), and press any button on it: the
browser only shows Control a controller once a button has been pressed. The setting says **Connected** and its
name. That first press does nothing.

| On the controller | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Left stick**, held | Jogs X and Y, in eight directions, at the **Fast XY** speed |
| **Right stick**, up or down, held | Jogs Z at the **Fast Z** speed |
| **LB**, held with a stick | Slow: a fifth of the speed, for creeping up on an edge |
| **D-pad** | One step in X or Y, of the size chosen in the jog panel (1 mm when that's **Fast**) |
| **Y** / **A** | One step up / down in Z |
| **X** | The next step size: 0.025, 0.1, 1, 10 mm |
| **B** | Stop: cancels a jog. During a job, **Hold** |

- **Let go and it stops.** A held jog is cancelled the moment the stick comes back past a quarter of its travel,
  and goes no further than the machine's travel allows, as the jog panel's do.
- **One direction at a time.** Push the stick a new way while jogging and the jog stops: bring the stick back to
  the middle, then push again. The same after anything else stops a jog (B, the window losing focus, the page
  going into the background, the controller switched off).
- **It jogs only when the jog panel could:** connected and idle, not during a job except when it's stopped at a
  tool change, and not while probing or running a quick action.
- **It can't start anything.** No Start, no Resume, no zeroing and no spindle from the controller: a knocked button
  only ever jogs or stops. Resume a held job, and set zero, on the screen.

### Spindle type and spin-up

Trim router or VFD spindle, and how long to wait after the spindle starts (M3 or M4) before any cutting move.
Most G-code files don't include that wait, so Control adds one after each spindle start. Carbide Motion does
the same, at about 2.5 s, which suits a router. A VFD spindle takes longer to reach speed: 7 s is the default
here. Too short, and the bit enters the material before it's up to speed.

### Start and stop high

Keeps the tool high whenever it's travelling, not cutting. Leave it on. At the start of a job and after every
tool change, the machine moves to the next XY position at the top of its travel before it plunges. Before every
spindle stop, and at the end of the job, it lifts clear first. This is what stops a bit dragging across clamps
and stock. A lift is skipped when the tool is already higher than the traverse height, so it never moves down.

### Traverse height

How far below the top of Z travel the machine sits when it's moving, not cutting, in the machine's own
coordinates. It's used by Start and stop high, Go to XY zero, the BitSetter's moves and the end of the job.
5 mm below the top is a good default; make it more if you have tall clamps.

### Tool-change and end-of-job positions

Two separate places the machine goes when you need to reach it.

**Tool change:** it lifts to near the top of Z travel and moves here before asking for the new tool. With
nothing captured it uses the front centre of the machine, worked out from the controller's travel, which is the
easiest place to reach. Pick another preset or capture a position to change that.

**End of a job:** as in Carbide Motion, it lifts, stops the spindle, returns to the job's XY zero, then parks.
With nothing captured it parks at the back centre of the machine, out of your way; capture a position to park
somewhere else.

Both use the machine's own coordinates, so they need a homed machine.

### Jog speeds

The feed rates the jog buttons and arrow keys use in Fast mode, for XY and for Z separately. They apply to
jogging only and have no effect on a running job. Keep Z slower than XY: it's the axis that finds clamps.
