Everything in 454 Design around the drawing tools themselves: setting up the job, layers, your tools, the
Toolpaths panel, checking the drawing, and Settings. For the drawing tools, see
[Design tools](design-tools.html); for every toolpath type and setting, the [CAM reference](cam-reference.html).

## The screen

<Shot src={overview} alt="454 Design with a workshop sign drawn: the tool groups on the left, the drawing with its toolpaths in the middle, and the Toolpaths panel on the right with four toolpaths." caption="A workshop sign with four toolpaths: drilling, a pocket, a V-carve and an outside profile." />

- **The header:** **Docs**, the **Tool library**, **Job setup**, **Settings**, and **454 Control**.
- **On the left:** the **Tools** tab holds the [tool groups](design-tools.html), and the **Layers** tab the
  [layers](#layers). **«** at the bottom of the tabs folds the panel away, leaving just the tabs; click
  **»** or either tab to open it again.
- **In the middle:** the drawing, on the material. Scroll to zoom, drag with the right or middle mouse
  button to move around, and press <kbd>Home</kbd> to fit the material in the window.
- **On the right:** the [Toolpaths panel](#the-toolpaths-panel). **»** collapses it to a narrow strip;
  click the strip to open it again. Fold both away for the most drawing room. Each stays as you left it.
- **Along the bottom of the drawing:** the [History bar](#history), every change you've made, to go back to.
- **Along the bottom:** the [status bar](#the-status-bar).

## Job setup

The material you're cutting, and where the machine's zero will be. Everything else is measured from
these, so it's worth setting first. Open it from the header, or **Settings → Job setup…**.

<Shot src={jobSetup} alt="The Job setup panel: width 320 and height 200 in millimetres; an XY zero picker with four corners and the centre, front-left chosen; thickness 12 mm with Z zero on top of the material; and the machine's cutting area, where the material sits on it, and how far in from that corner." />

- **Width, Height and units.** The units apply everywhere: the material, dimensions, typed values, the
  measure tool and the readout. A unit typed after a number always wins, so `25mm` works in an inch
  drawing.
- **XY zero:** where X0 Y0 is on the material: one of the four corners, or the centre. Match it to where
  you'll set X and Y zero on the machine in 454 Control. Changing it keeps your parts where they are on the
  material.
- **Thickness** and **Z zero:** the top of the material, or the spoilboard under it. The thickness is used
  whenever something cuts right through, so measure it rather than trusting the label. Z zero must match
  where you zero the tool in 454 Control.
- **Machine:** the machine's cutting area (from 454 Control, a Shapeoko or Nomad size, or your own), which
  corner the material sits against, and how far in. It's used to draw the cutting area (**Guides → Machine
  area**), so you can see how the material and parts sit on the bed.

## Layers

The **Layers** tab, on the left. Layers keep a drawing organised, and let you hide or lock parts of it
while you work on the rest.

<Shot src={layers} alt="The Layers tab: three layers. Reference is hidden and locked, shown struck through; Engraving holds one shape; Outline, the active layer, holds seven and is outlined." />

- **The active layer** is outlined: new shapes go on it. Click a layer to make it active.
- **The eye** shows or hides a layer. Hidden layers are drawn struck through, and their shapes can't be
  selected.
- **The lock** stops a layer's shapes being selected or changed, while still showing them.
- **The number** is how many shapes the layer holds. **Double-click** a name to rename it.
- **With shapes selected**, a layer offers to move them onto it.
- **The bin** deletes a layer; the last one can't be deleted. **+ Layer** adds one.

Layers and [sheets](#sheets) are separate: every sheet has every layer.

## Sheets

A project that needs more than one piece of material has a **sheet** for each: the sides of a cabinet on one,
the shelves on another. The **Sheet** bar is at the top of the Toolpaths panel.

<Shot src={sheetsBar} alt="The Sheet bar: the word Sheet, a list showing Sign (4 toolpaths), and three buttons: a plus, a pencil and a bin." caption="The Sheet bar: choose the sheet, add one, rename it or delete it." />

- **Add a sheet** with the **+** beside the sheet's name. The first time, everything drawn so far becomes
  **Sheet 1**, and the new **Sheet 2** is shown, empty.
- **One sheet is shown at a time.** Choose it from the list. Its shapes, dimensions and toolpaths are the only
  ones you see, select, snap to, trim against, nest, check and export.
- **New shapes go on the sheet being shown.** To move shapes to another sheet, cut them (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>X</kbd>),
  change sheets, and paste in place (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd>). Their dimensions come with
  them; make their toolpaths again on the new sheet.
- **Each sheet has its own toolpaths and its own G-code.** **Save G-code** saves the sheet being shown, in a
  file named for it (`Cabinet - Sides.nc`), so a file can never put two sheets' parts on one piece of
  material. A toolpath can't take shapes from two sheets. The job sheet and the previews cover the sheet being
  shown too.
- **The pencil** renames the sheet. **The bin** deletes it, with its shapes, dimensions and toolpaths, after
  asking; <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> brings it back. The last sheet can't be deleted.
- **Every sheet uses the material in Job setup**: the same size, thickness and zero. [Parameters](design-tools.html#parameters)
  and layers are shared by all the sheets, so a toolpath template applies to each sheet by its layer names.

A multi-sheet VCarve project opens with its sheets, named as in VCarve.

## The tool library

Your bits, their tool numbers, and their feeds and speeds for each material. Open it from the header. It's
shared with 454 Control.

<Shot src={library} alt="The tool library: five tools in My tools (two end mills, two V-bits and a ball nose), with the 6.35 mm end mill selected, showing its diameter, flutes, notes, tool number T201, and feeds, plunge, spindle speed, stepdown and stepover for hardwood, softwood, MDF and plywood, with hardwood highlighted as the chosen material." />

- **Import VCarve library…** brings in a VCarve tool database (`.vtdb`), with its folders, tool numbers,
  and feeds and speeds for each machine and material. Or build your own: **+ Tool** adds a bit, and
  **+ Material** a material.
- **Select a tool** to see its geometry, its tool number on each machine, and its feeds and speeds for each
  material. **Edit**, **Duplicate** or **Delete** it there.
- **Choose the material** by clicking its row in a tool's **Feeds and speeds** list: it's highlighted, and new
  toolpaths take that material's feeds and speeds. Every material in the library is listed, so a material the tool
  has none for yet says so; choose it and **Edit** to add them. While editing a tool, the materials are buttons
  above its feeds and speeds. **Machine**, at the top, chooses which machine's feeds and speeds are shown.
- **Search tools** finds a bit by name. Tools you've used lately gather in a **Recently used** folder at
  the top.

## The Toolpaths panel

Where a drawing becomes a job. Toolpaths are in beta: check every job in 454 Control's preview, and run it
in the air, before cutting material.

<Shot src={tpPanel} alt="The Toolpaths panel: the beta note, then four toolpath cards (Drilling, Pocket, V-carve and Outside profile), each with its tool, feed, depth and details, and buttons to reorder, recalculate, edit and delete; then Save G-code and Job sheet." />

<Steps>

1. Select the shapes to cut, then press **+** (New toolpath) at the top of the panel, or the <kbd>+</kbd> key.
2. In the editor, choose what kind of cut it is, the tool and the depth, and check the rest. Nothing is worked
   out while you change settings, as in VCarve, so typing never lags; editing a toolpath, the drawing shows it
   as it was last worked out.
3. Press **Create**. The toolpath is worked out, with a **Working…** panel while it is, and gets a card in the
   panel. If none of the shapes selected can be cut (open lines for a pocket, say), or none are selected, it says
   so and makes nothing; if only some can, it says which were left out.
4. When they're all made, press **Preview in 454 Control** to check the job in Control's 3D preview, then
   **Save G-code…** for the file to run, and **Job sheet…** for
   a printable page to keep at the machine.

</Steps>

**The editor** holds everything about one toolpath: its shapes, the kind of cut, the tool (its diameter
comes from the tool), the depth or **through**, the depth per pass, and the settings that kind of cut uses,
such as the stepover and clearing pattern for a pocket, the direction, and a finishing pass. Nothing is
guessed: **Create** stays disabled until you've chosen a tool and a depth.

<Shot src={editor} alt="The toolpath editor for a pocket: 2 shapes, Pocket (clear the area), a 6.35 mm cutter, cut depth 6 mm, 1 mm per pass, 40 percent stepover, offset rings clearing, feed 800 mm/min, climb direction, and finishing, with Cancel and Update." />

**Each card** shows the toolpath's number in the cutting order, its tool and feed, and a summary of its
depth and details. On each card:

- **The tick box** includes it in the G-code, or leaves it out. **The eye** shows or hides its preview.
- **The arrows** move it earlier or later in the cutting order; you can also drag a card by its handle.
- **Recalculate (↻)** rebuilds it from its shapes and the material as they are now. It's highlighted when
  the toolpath is out of date. **Recalculate all**, at the top of the panel, does every toolpath.
- **The pencil** opens it in the editor; **the bin** deletes it.
- **Right-click** a card for the same actions in a menu.

At the top of the panel: **Check** (below), **Recalculate all**, **+** for a new toolpath, and **»** to
collapse the panel. A VCarve project with toolpaths offers to make them editable here.

## Checking the drawing

**Check**, at the top of the Toolpaths panel, looks the drawing over before you cut: open outlines, stray
pieces, shapes drawn twice, outlines that cross themselves, and shapes no toolpath cuts.

<Shot src={check} alt="A dialog: 2 things need a look before cutting. One shape is drawn twice, on top of another, and Delete can fix this. Two shapes are open; profiles and pockets need closed outlines. The shapes concerned are selected on the drawing. Buttons: Cancel, and Fix what can be fixed." />

The shapes concerned are selected on the drawing, so you can see them. **Fix what can be fixed** does what
it safely can, such as deleting a shape drawn twice, and says what's left for you. A drawing with nothing
to report says so.

## Settings

The gear in the header.

<Shot src={settings} alt="The Settings panel: Job setup; the theme set to Dark and the accent to Gold; and the colours for vectors, toolpath previews, construction lines and dimensions, with Show dimensions layer ticked." />

- **Job setup…** opens [Job setup](#job-setup).
- **Theme** and **Accent:** dark or light, and the accent colour. They're shared with 454 Control, so both
  apps look the same.
- **Display colours** for the drawing's vectors, toolpath previews, construction lines and dimensions, and
  whether to show the dimensions.
- **Snap lines to 45° and 90°**, on unless you turn it off: lines and polylines snap to 0°, 45°, 90° and
  so on as you draw them.

## History

Every change you make is a step on the **History bar**, along the bottom of the drawing, oldest on the left:
**Rectangle**, **Fillet corners 6 mm**, **Offset 3 mm**, **Toolpath Pocket 1**. It's the same list Undo goes back
through, but you can see it and jump straight to any step.

- **Click a step** to see the drawing as it was just after it, toolpaths and all. The steps after it stay on the
  bar, faded, and the step you're on is underlined. Click a later step to come forward again; nothing is lost
  by looking. **Start** is the drawing before the first step.
- **Undo** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>) and **Redo** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Y</kbd>) move one step along the same line.
- **Changing something while you're back at an earlier step** carries on from there, and drops the faded steps
  after it, as Undo always has. The bar says so while you're back: "Back 2 steps: a change here drops the 2
  after it".
- **The history is saved with the drawing.** Save puts it in the drawing's file, and opening the file brings it
  back, so you can go back to how a project was last week. It's kept between visits too, with the drawing.
- **Opening a drawing, or starting a new one, is a step** (in bold), so you can go back to the drawing you had
  open before. A file keeps only its own steps.
- **Hover over a step** for when it was made. Scroll the mouse wheel over the bar to move along a long history,
  and click **History** to fold the bar away, or open it again.

The last 500 steps are kept.

### Changing a step

As in Fusion, you can go back and change a step, and everything after it is done again.

1. **Double-click the step** on the History bar. A small panel shows its numbers.
2. Change them, and click **Change** (or press <kbd>Enter</kbd>).
3. Every step after it is done again, on the drawing as it now is. With a long way to go, the **Working** panel
   shows how far it's got.

What can be changed:

| Step | Its numbers |
| --- | --- |
| Rectangle | X, Y, width, height |
| Circle | centre X and Y, diameter |
| Line | start and end |
| Text | the text, its height |
| Fillet (one corner), Fillet corners | the radius |
| Offset | the distance |
| Move | how far across and up |
| Resize, Rotate, and moves typed into the selection box | the size, angle or position typed |
| Scale, Stretch and Rotate by dragging the selection's handles | how much, across and up, or the angle |

How the steps after it are done again:

- **Fillets, offsets, Mirror, Weld, Subtract and Intersect, moves, resizes and rotations are done again by the
  tool,** on the shapes they worked on, as those shapes are now. Make a rectangle wider and its rounded corners are
  rounded again at the new size; an offset or a mirror copy of it follows it; a weld it was part of is welded
  again; a move still moves it by the same amount. The shapes a step made keep their identity, so the steps after
  it still find them, and toolpaths cut the shapes as they now are. Mirror and Weld have no numbers of their own,
  but follow the steps before them.
- **A rounded corner is the same corner of the outline,** counted round it. If the outline has a different number
  of corners by then, the fillet is red rather than rounding the wrong one.
- **Anything else is done again as it was:** a shape drawn is drawn again, a shape deleted is deleted again, and a
  change to a shape is made again, but only if that shape is still exactly as the step found it.
- **A step that can't be done again turns red** and is skipped: the shape it works on is gone (an offset too big
  to leave a shape, say), or it changed a shape that's now different, such as a point dragged by hand on an outline
  that's now a different size. 454 never guesses what you meant. Hover over a red step to see why. A later change
  that makes it possible again brings it back.

Changing a step isn't a step of its own: to put it back, change it again.

## The status bar

<Shot src={statusBar} alt="The status bar: the pointer's position, X and Y, on the left; the current tool's instructions in the middle; and a bulb for recent messages on the right." />

- **X and Y** on the left: where the pointer is on the drawing.
- **The middle** says what the current tool wants next, and what you can type.
- **The bulb** on the right shows recent messages: everything Design has told you, in case one disappeared
  before you read it.
