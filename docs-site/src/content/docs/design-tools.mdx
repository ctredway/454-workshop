Every tool in the panel on the left of 454 Design, group by group: what each one does and how to use it. Hover over any tool in Design for a short reminder of the same. For a walkthrough from a blank drawing to G-code, see the [Design quick start](design-quickstart.html).

**Groups:** [File](#file) · [Create vectors](#create-vectors) · [Edit vectors](#edit-vectors) · [Align and nest](#align-and-nest) · [Guides](#guides) · [View and history](#view-and-history)

## Before you start: typing exact values

Most tools can be used with the mouse alone, but for exact work you can type values at any time: just start typing while a tool is active, and a box appears at the pointer. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to use the value. Values are in the drawing’s units unless you type a unit after the number (`3mm`, `0.5in`).

| Type | Means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `x,y` | a point, measured from the drawing’s zero | `50,30` |
| `@dx,dy` | a point relative to the last one | `@10,0` is 10 to the right |
| `length<angle` | a distance at an angle from the last point (degrees, counter-clockwise from the right) | `50<45` |

<Shot src={typing} alt="Drawing a line: a dashed line runs from the last point to the pointer, and a box beside it holds the typed value @40,0." caption="Typing @40,0 while drawing a line. The dashed line follows the pointer; press Enter and the next point goes 40 mm to the right of the last one instead." />

**Snapping:** while drawing, the pointer snaps to the ends, midpoints, corners and centers of shapes, to guides and where guides cross, and to the grid. Lines and polylines, vectors and construction lines alike, also snap to 0°, 45°, 90° and so on from their start: a dashed line and the angle show when they do, and holding <kbd>Shift</kbd> locks to the nearest one. (A shape's point still wins over the angle.) **Esc** cancels whatever you’re in the middle of, and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> undoes almost anything.

## File

Starting, saving and opening drawings, and handing them to other software.

<Shot src={gFile} alt="The File group: New, Save, Save as, Load, Recover last drawing, Export DXF and Export SVG." />

### New

Clears all shapes and guides, to start a fresh drawing.

1. Click **New**.
2. Changed your mind? <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> brings everything back. If the drawing hadn't been saved
   to a file, **Recover last drawing** can bring it back later too.

### Save

Saves the drawing, with its toolpaths, to a `.454.json` file you can open again later.

1. Click **Save** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>S</kbd>). The first time, choose where to put the file. After that, Save
   saves straight back to the same file, without asking.
2. **Save as** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>S</kbd>) always asks: to save a copy under another name, or
   somewhere else. Save then goes to the new file.
3. Keep it with the job’s G-code, or as a backup.

**In the desktop app**, a drawing you open (with **Load**, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>O</kbd>, or by dropping it on the
window) saves back to its own file, and 454 Design remembers which file it is: <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>S</kbd> still
saves there after you close 454 Design and open it again. If the file has been moved or deleted, Save asks
where instead. **In a browser**, Save goes back to the file for as long as the page stays open (Chrome and
Edge; other browsers download the file each time).

Design also keeps the drawing in the browser (or the desktop app) as you work, so it's there when you come
back. That isn't a file, though: when you close Design with a drawing that hasn't been saved to a file since
it last changed, it asks first. The desktop app asks **Save…**, **Don't save** or **Cancel**; a browser
shows its own "Leave site?" warning. Exporting a DXF or SVG doesn't count as saving the drawing.

### Load

Opens a saved drawing or a VCarve project, adds DXF and SVG files to the drawing, or traces an image.

1. Click **Load** and pick a file, or drag it onto Design's window. DXF and SVG files can be picked or dropped several at once.
2. **A saved drawing** (`.454.json`) opens as it was.
3. **A VCarve project** (`.crv`) opens a panel: choose whether to bring in the **Drawing vectors** and the **Toolpath preview vectors**, and millimetres or inches. The material size comes across too, and the project's toolpaths can be made editable in Design afterwards.
4. **DXF and SVG files** open a panel:
   - **Into:** **Add to this drawing** (the default when something's drawn) keeps everything that's there, toolpaths included; **Replace the drawing** starts again with just the file's shapes.
   - **Place:** **Beside what's drawn**, **At X0 Y0**, or **Where the file has them**. Several files go side by side.
   - **Scale to width** (one file at a time), and for a DXF, whether its numbers are millimetres or inches.

   The new shapes come in **selected**, ready to drag into place. The file's layers join layers of the same name, and new names become new layers. A DXF keeps its true curves. An SVG comes in at its real size, from its own units; its curves, circles, rectangles and Inkscape layers come across, but text doesn't: convert text to paths in your editor first. An SVG saved by Design comes back exactly where it was.
5. **An image** (PNG, JPG, GIF, BMP or WebP) opens the **Trace image** panel: set the **Threshold** (or press **Auto**), **Invert** for light artwork on a dark background, the **Smoothing**, specks to ignore, and the traced width, then press **Trace**. **Fit curves** turns the outlines into smooth arcs and lines with far fewer points.

### Recover last drawing

Brings back the last drawing that was put aside without being saved to a file: one closed with **Don't
save**, cleared with **New**, or replaced by opening another drawing. One is kept at a time, the most recent.

1. Click **Recover last drawing**. Pointing at it shows when that drawing was put aside. It's greyed out
   when there's nothing to recover.
2. If the drawing that's open hasn't been saved either, the two swap places, so neither is lost: Recover
   again swaps them back. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> undoes a recover.

### Export DXF

Saves the drawing as a DXF file, for VCarve, Fusion, a laser, or other CAD and CAM software.

1. Click **Export DXF** and choose where to save it.
2. Shapes keep their true curves and their layers; everything is in millimetres.

### Export SVG

Saves the drawing as an SVG file, for laser software, vinyl cutters and Inkscape.

1. Click **Export SVG** and choose where to save it.
2. It’s true to size in millimetres, with true curves and your layers.

## Create vectors

Selecting, and drawing new shapes.

<Shot src={gCreate} alt="The Create vectors group: Select, Line, Rectangle, Circle, Arc, Polyline and Text." />

### Select / move

Shortcut: <kbd>V</kbd>

Picks shapes so you can move them, change them, or use another tool on them.

1. **Click** a shape to select it; **Shift-click** to add or remove shapes.
2. **Drag a box** on empty space: left to right selects shapes entirely inside it; right to left also selects any shape it touches.
3. **Drag** a selected shape to move it, or nudge it with the arrow keys: 1 mm a press, 0.1 mm with <kbd>Shift</kbd>.
4. **Double-click** a selected shape for handles: corners scale in proportion, sides stretch one way. Click it again for rotate handles.
5. <kbd>Delete</kbd> removes what’s selected.
6. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd> copies what’s selected, <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>X</kbd> cuts it, and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd>
   pastes it: see [Copy and paste](#copy-and-paste).

:::tip
Shapes on hidden or locked layers can’t be selected.
:::

#### Exact position, size and rotation

While shapes are selected, the **Selection box** at the top of the drawing shows where they are and how big,
and takes exact values, like VCarve's Move, Set Size and Rotate:

- **The small grid** on its left picks the reference point: a corner, the middle of a side, or the centre.
  X and Y are where that point is, and it stays put when you resize or rotate.
- **X** and **Y**: type where the reference point should be, and press Enter. The selection moves there.
- **W** and **H**: type the width or height. With the padlock closed, the other changes in proportion; click the
  padlock to set them separately. Stretching a circle one way makes an ellipse.
- **Rotate**: type the angle in degrees and press Enter to turn the selection about the reference point:
  positive anticlockwise, negative clockwise. A rectangle turned 90° stays a rectangle.

Values are in the units the drawing is shown in (millimetres or inches). <kbd>Esc</kbd> puts back what
you typed; <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> undoes a change.

### Copy and paste

Copies shapes, with their dimensions, to paste somewhere else in the drawing, or into another drawing.

1. Select the shapes, then press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>C</kbd> to copy them, or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>X</kbd> to cut them
   (copy, then remove).
2. Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd> to paste: the copy lands centred on the pointer, and snaps as drawing does. If the
   pointer isn't over the drawing, it goes 10 mm right and down from the original.
3. Or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>V</kbd> to paste it exactly where it was copied from, then move it
   precisely: type an offset with **Copy** or **Select**, or use the arrow keys.

The pasted shapes come in selected. Paste again for another copy. In the desktop app, **Edit → Copy**, **Cut**
and **Paste** do the same.

- **Dimensions come along.** Every dimension that measures only copied shapes is pasted measuring the
  copies: a rectangle's width, or the distance between two parts you copied together. Change a copy through
  its dimension and the original stays as it was. A dimension to a shape you didn't copy stays with the
  original.
- **Toolpaths don't.** They belong to the original shapes: add the copies to a toolpath, or make a new one.
- **Layers:** a copy goes on its own layer, or on the current layer if its own is hidden or locked.
- What's copied stays available after you close 454 Design, and in another 454 Design window. In a text box,
  the keys copy and paste text as usual.

<Shot src={selHandles} alt="A selected rectangle with square scale handles at its corners and sides, and its width and height shown; a circle beside it." caption="Double-click a selected shape for its handles: corners scale in proportion, sides stretch one way." />

### Line

Shortcut: <kbd>L</kbd>

Draws straight lines, one after another.

1. Click the first point, or type it as `x,y`.
2. Click the next point, or type it: `x,y`, `@dx,dy` or `length<angle`. Each click adds a line from the last point. Near 0°, 45°, 90° and so on, the line snaps to that angle; hold <kbd>Shift</kbd> to lock it to the nearest one.
3. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> when you’re done.

### Rectangle

Shortcut: <kbd>R</kbd>

Draws a rectangle.

1. Click one corner, or type it as `x,y`.
2. Click the opposite corner, or type the size as `width,height`, such as `120,80`.

### Circle

Shortcut: <kbd>C</kbd>

Draws a circle.

1. Click the center, or type it as `x,y`.
2. Click a point on the edge, or type the size: `D5` for a 5 mm diameter, `R2.5` for a 2.5 mm radius.

### Ellipse

Draws an ellipse, square to the material. Rotate it afterwards if it should lean.

1. Click the centre, or type it as `x,y`.
2. Click a corner of the box it fits in, or type its width and height, such as `120,60`.

An ellipse is made of arcs that stay within 0.0005 mm of a true ellipse, far finer than any bit cuts, so it
offsets, cuts and exports like any other outline. Its points can be moved with Edit nodes, but it doesn't
stay an ellipse you can resize by typing, as a rectangle does.

### Polygon

Draws a regular polygon: a triangle, square, hexagon, octagon, and so on.

1. The prompt shows the number of sides. To change it, type `S` and the number, such as `S8`, and press Enter. It's remembered for next time.
2. Click the centre, or type it as `x,y`.
3. Click where one corner goes, or type the size: `R40` for 40 mm from the centre to each corner, `D80` for 80 mm across the corners, or `F70` for 70 mm across the flats (the size a spanner fits). A typed polygon stands on a flat.

### Star

Draws a star.

1. The prompt shows the number of points. To change it, type `S` and the number, such as `S6`, and press Enter.
2. Click the centre, or type it as `x,y`.
3. Click the tip of one point, or type `R40` for points 40 mm from the centre, with one pointing straight up.
4. Move the pointer in or out to set how deep the points are, and click; or type the inner corners' distance from the centre, such as `15`.

### Arc

Shortcut: <kbd>A</kbd>

Draws an arc through a start and end point.

1. Click the start point, or type it.
2. Click the end point, or type it (`x,y`, `@dx,dy` or `length<angle`).
3. Move the pointer to bow the arc and click to place it, or type a radius: a positive radius bows it to the left of the line from start to end, a negative one to the right.

### Polyline

Shortcut: <kbd>P</kbd>

Draws a connected outline of straight lines, open or closed.

1. Click the first point, or type it.
2. Click each next point, or type it (`@dx,dy` and `length<angle` work too).
3. Click the first point again to close the shape, or press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to leave it open.

### Text

Shortcut: <kbd>K</kbd>

Adds text, in any of Design’s fonts or one from your computer.

1. Click where the text should start: the bottom of the first letter.
2. In the **Add text** panel, type the text, and choose the **Font** (or **Use font file…** for a `.ttf`, `.otf` or `.woff`), the **Letter height** and the **Align**ment.
3. Press **Place text** to add it as text you can edit later, or **Convert to curves** to add it as ordinary shapes.
4. To edit text later, click it with the Text tool, or double-click it twice with Select.

:::tip
For text you'll cut all the way through, choose a font from the **Stencil** group: they keep the middles of letters like O, A and R attached, so they don't fall out.
:::

## Edit vectors

Changing shapes you’ve drawn: corners, trimming, offsets, copies, arrangement and more. Tools that act on a selection need something selected first: press <kbd>V</kbd>, pick the shapes, then choose the tool.

<Shot src={gEdit} alt="The Edit vectors group: Fillet, Trim, Extend, Offset, Edit nodes, Dimension, Copy, Mirror, the flips, Rotate, Array, Group, Ungroup, Join, Explode and Convert to curves." />

### Fillet

Shortcut: <kbd>F</kbd>

Rounds a corner, or cuts a relief into it so a square part fits a CNC-cut pocket. It can also put a rounded corner back to sharp.

1. Set the size in the **Radius** box at the bottom of the drawing, or just start typing a number.
2. Choose the corner type beside it: **Round**, **Dog-bone** (a relief at the corner, for the bit’s radius), or **T-bone** (a relief into one edge; click nearer the edge it should cut into).
3. Move over a corner: a green check and a preview show what a click will do. Click to apply it. This works on outline and rectangle corners, and (with **Round**) where two separate lines meet.
4. To make a rounded corner sharp again, click the rounded corner: the sharp corner that will come back is shown dashed in amber first.

<Shot src={filletHover} alt="A rectangle with one corner already rounded; hovering another corner shows a green check and a preview of the fillet." caption="Hovering a sharp corner previews the fillet before you click." />

<Shot src={filletBar} alt="The prompt bar: instructions, the Radius box reading 12.00 mm, and the Round, Dog-bone and T-bone buttons." caption="The Radius box and corner types, along the bottom of the drawing while Fillet is active." />

### Trim

Shortcut: <kbd>T</kbd>

Removes a piece of a line, arc or circle, cut off where other shapes cross it.

1. Click the piece you want to remove. The crossings on either side mark where it’s cut.

### Extend

Shortcut: <kbd>E</kbd>

Lengthens a line or arc until it meets the next shape.

1. Click near the end you want to lengthen. It runs out to the next shape in that direction.

### Offset

Shortcut: <kbd>O</kbd>

Makes a copy of shapes a set distance outside or inside them, like a border.

1. Select the shapes, or click them after choosing Offset.
2. In the **Offset** panel, type the **Distance**, and click **Direction** to switch between outward and inward. A preview shows the result.
3. Press **OK**. You can also type the distance directly: `+2` for 2 mm outward, `-1.5` for inward.

:::tip
For open shapes, such as a single line, a positive distance goes to the left of the direction it was drawn in.
:::

<Shot src={offset} alt="The Offset panel with a distance of 8 mm outward, and a dashed preview 8 mm outside the selected rectangle." caption="The dashed outline shows where the offset will go before you press OK." />

### Edit nodes

Shortcut: <kbd>N</kbd>

Moves the individual points of an outline, or sets them exactly.

1. Click an outline to show its points.
2. **Drag** a point to move it.
3. **Click a point** to type its exact position as `x,y`.
4. **Click a segment** to type its length; the later point moves along the segment.
5. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> when you’re done.

### Dimension

Shortcut: <kbd>D</kbd>

Sets exact distances and sizes, and keeps a dimension on the drawing that updates as things change.

1. **For a distance between two shapes:** click the edge of the shape to move, then the edge to measure from (that shape stays put). Type the distance and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. Start it with `E` to measure edge to edge.
2. **For a shape's own size:** click a rectangle's side twice for its width or height (or a line for its length, a circle for its diameter, an arc for its radius), type the size, and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>.
3. To change a dimension later, click its value. To remove one, click it and press the &#10005; beside the value.

**A distance on each side stretches the shape.** When a shape is already held by a distance on its opposite side, a new distance moves just the edge you clicked, instead of the whole shape. So to place a rectangle 10&nbsp;mm inside another on both sides: set one side's distance, which moves it into place, then the other's, which stretches it to fit. Changing either one later keeps the other. The same works for straight-edged outlines.

**Dimensions follow their edges.** A dimension remembers which edges it measures, so it keeps measuring them when shapes are moved or stretched. A rectangle's size keeps any side held by a distance where it is; if both sides are held, it says so rather than breaking one of them.

:::tip
Selecting two shapes and pressing <kbd>D</kbd> works the same way: click each shape on the edge you mean when you select it.
:::

### Parameters

The **fx** button, beside Dimension. Parameters are named sizes, saved with the drawing: `thickness`,
`dado`, `shelf_gap`. Use one in a dimension, and when you change the parameter, every dimension using it
follows, so a change of plywood is one edit instead of a dozen.

1. Press **fx**, type a name and a value, and press **Add**. A value can be a number (`18.3`, `3/4`), or
   arithmetic using other parameters: `thickness + 0.2`, `(height - 3 * thickness) / 2`.
2. In a dimension, type the parameter's name, or arithmetic with it (`dado * 2`), instead of a number. The
   dimension shows it, like **18.50 = dado**.
3. Change a value in the list, and every dimension using it, and the shapes they drive, follow. One
   <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> takes the whole change back.

**`material`** is always there: the material's thickness from Settings. A dado that's `material + 0.2`
follows when you change the sheet you're cutting.

- **Units:** numbers mean the units the drawing showed when you typed them, and a number can say its own:
  `18mm`, `3/4in`, `3/4"`. Each parameter keeps its units, so switching the drawing between mm and
  inches doesn't change any sizes.
- **Typing a plain number** into a linked dimension unlinks it. Measurements typed as before still work:
  `12 1/2` is twelve and a half, and `E` still means edge to edge.
- **Renaming** a parameter changes it everywhere it's used. One that's in use can't be deleted until the
  dimensions and parameters using it are changed.
- **Mistakes are refused**, with what's wrong: a name that doesn't exist, a bracket left open, or a
  parameter that would depend on itself. Nothing changes until it's put right.
- **In toolpaths and other boxes:** a toolpath's depth and tabs can use parameters too, and follow them
  (see [the CAM reference](cam-reference#the-basics)). Every other length box, such as the selection box's
  W and H, or an offset, takes a parameter or arithmetic (`600 - 2 * thickness`) and works it out once.
- **Only what's dimensioned follows.** A dimension sets the size or distance it measures, nothing more: if a
  shelf should stay against a side when the sides move, give it a distance to that side too. A dimension
  that's been moved by hand, or can't be what its parameters say, is marked **&#9888;**.

### Copy

Shortcut: <kbd>M</kbd>

Makes a copy of the selection somewhere else.

1. Select the shapes first.
2. Click a base point on them, or type the offset as `@dx,dy`.
3. Click where the base point should go, or type it as `x,y` or `@dx,dy`.

### Mirror

Shortcut: <kbd>Shift+M</kbd>

Makes a mirrored copy of shapes, as if seen in a mirror along a line.

1. In the **Mirror** panel, click **Objects** and pick the shapes, then click **Mirror line** and pick the line to mirror across. A construction line is used as the mirror line automatically (to make a line a construction line, select it and press <kbd>X</kbd>).
2. Or click two points to set a mirror line of your own.
3. Press **OK** to make the mirrored copy.

:::tip
To turn shapes over where they are, without a copy, use Flip horizontal or Flip vertical.
:::

### Flip horizontal

Shortcut: <kbd>Shift+H</kbd>

Turns the selection over left to right, where it is.

1. Select the shapes, then click **Flip horizontal**.

### Flip vertical

Shortcut: <kbd>Shift+V</kbd>

Turns the selection over top to bottom, where it is.

1. Select the shapes, then click **Flip vertical**.

### Rotate

Shortcut: <kbd>Shift+R</kbd>

Turns the selection by an exact angle.

1. Select the shapes first.
2. Click the point to turn around, or type it as `x,y`.
3. Type the angle in degrees and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>: positive turns counter-clockwise, negative clockwise.

:::tip
To turn shapes by eye instead, double-click them twice with Select for rotate handles.
:::

### Array

Shortcut: <kbd>Y</kbd>

Makes several evenly spaced copies, in a row or around a circle.

1. Select the shapes first.
2. **In a row:** type how many and the step between them as `count@dx,dy`; `5@12,0` makes 5 copies 12 mm apart to the right.
3. **Around a circle:** click the center, then type how many and the angle between them as `count<angle`; `6<60` makes 6 copies 60° apart.

### Group

Shortcut: <kbd>Ctrl+G</kbd>

Bundles the selected shapes so they select and move as one piece. Nothing about the shapes changes.

1. Select the shapes, then click **Group**.

### Ungroup

Shortcut: <kbd>Ctrl+U</kbd>

Breaks a group back into its separate shapes.

1. Select the group, then click **Ungroup**.

### Weld

Joins closed shapes that overlap or touch into one outline, and removes the lines inside. Use it for a sign
with a rounded end, a board with a handle, or letters that run into each other.

1. Select two or more closed shapes (rectangles, circles, closed outlines, groups or text).
2. Click **Weld**.

Shapes in the selection that don't overlap or touch anything else are left as they were. Holes stay holes:
a letter's middle, or a gap the shapes surround, comes out as its own outline.

### Subtract

Cuts the other selected shapes out of the biggest one, like cutting a notch or a finger hole out of a part.

1. Select the part and the shapes to cut out of it.
2. Click **Subtract**.

The biggest shape is the one kept. If two shapes are the same size, such as two circles for a crescent moon,
the one you picked first is kept: click it, then Shift+click the other. A shape wholly inside the part becomes
a hole in it. Selected shapes that don't overlap the part are left alone.

### Intersect

Keeps only the area that all the selected shapes share, and removes the rest.

1. Select the shapes.
2. Click **Intersect**.

If the shapes don't all overlap, nothing changes and Design says so.

:::tip
All three keep the shapes exact: straight edges stay straight and curves stay true arcs, so the result
measures, offsets and cuts just as if you had drawn it. The result is selected afterwards, and **Ctrl+Z**
undoes it. A toolpath that cut one of the shapes cuts the result instead (for Subtract, the part that was kept);
check it before cutting. Open shapes such as lines take no part.
:::

### Join

Joins open shapes whose ends meet into one continuous outline, such as lines drawn separately into a closed shape.

1. Select the open shapes, then click **Join**.

:::tip
Toolpaths that cut around a shape need a closed outline; Join is how separate pieces become one.
:::

### Explode

Breaks an outline into its separate lines and arcs.

1. Select the outline, then click **Explode**.

### Convert to curves

Turns text into ordinary shapes, so you can trim, offset or reshape the letters.

1. Select the text, then click **Convert to curves**.

:::tip
Once converted, the text can no longer be edited as text.
:::

### Text on curve

Lays text along a line, an arc, a circle or any outline, each letter turned to follow it: an arched sign, or
a name round a plate or a badge.

1. Add the text with the Text tool, and draw the curve.
2. Select the text and the curve (click one, then <kbd>Shift</kbd>+click the other).
3. Click **Text on curve**.

The text sits on top of the curve, reading left to right, centred. On a circle it goes across the top. The
curve stays in the drawing: keep it as a guide or cut, or delete it.

To change how it sits, double-click the text (or click it with the Text tool). The Text panel has, as well as
the words, font and height:

- **On the curve**: from the start, centred, or to the end. On a circle, that's measured from its top (its
  bottom, when reversed).
- **Gap**: space between the curve and the letters.
- **Letters**: standing on the curve, or hanging under it (tops towards it), such as text inside an arch.
- **Reverse**: runs the other way. On a circle, this puts the text along the bottom, still reading left to
  right, which is what a round badge needs.
- **Straighten** takes the text off the curve.

If the text is longer than the curve, the panel says so. Moving, turning or scaling the text takes its curve
with it; changing the letter height keeps the same curve.

## Align and nest

Arranging shapes: packing parts onto the material, centering, lining up and spacing. The tools that line shapes up use the **last one you picked** as the reference, outlined in green.

<Shot src={gAlign} alt="The Align and nest group: Nest parts, the centering tools, the edge alignment tools and even spacing." />

### Nest parts

Packs parts onto the material as tightly as it can, to save material, with as many copies as you ask for.

1. Select the parts to nest, or select nothing to nest everything.
2. In the **Nest parts** panel, set the **Copies of each**, the **Spacing** between parts, the **Edge margin** from the material’s edges, and the **Rotation**: any angle for the tightest fit, 90° steps, or none, to keep parts as drawn.
3. Press **Nest parts**. The parts are laid out on the material.

<Shot src={nestPanel} alt="The Nest parts panel: one 60 by 40 mm part with 1 copy, spacing 4 mm, edge margin 5 mm, and rotation set to any angle." />

### Center in material

Shortcut: <kbd>F9</kbd>

Moves the selection, as one block, to the middle of the material.

1. Select the shapes, then click **Center in material**.

### Center left-right in material

Moves the selection sideways to the middle of the material, leaving its height as it is.

1. Select the shapes, then click the tool.

### Center top-bottom in material

Moves the selection up or down to the middle of the material, leaving it where it is sideways.

1. Select the shapes, then click the tool.

### Center on last picked

Centers the other selected shapes on the last one you picked.

1. Select the shapes to move, then Shift-click the one to center them on last.
2. Click **Center on last picked**.

### Line up centers left-right

Moves shapes sideways so their centers line up with the last one picked.

1. Select the shapes, picking the one to line up with last, then click the tool.

### Line up centers top-bottom

Moves shapes up or down so their centers line up with the last one picked.

1. Select the shapes, picking the one to line up with last, then click the tool.

### Align left, right, top or bottom edges

Four tools that line up the shapes’ left, right, top or bottom edges with the last one picked.

1. Select the shapes, picking the one to line up with last, then click the edge to line up.

### Space evenly left to right, or top to bottom

Spaces shapes out with equal gaps between them. The first and last shapes stay where they are.

1. Select three or more shapes, then click the direction to space them in.

## Guides

Construction lines for laying out a drawing. They snap, but aren’t cut.

<Shot src={gGuides} alt="The Guides group: Offset guide, Remove all guides and Machine area." />

### Offset guide

Shortcut: <kbd>G</kbd>

Adds a guide line a set distance from an edge of the material or of a shape.

1. Click the edge to measure from: a material edge or a straight edge of the drawing.
2. Type the distance and press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. A negative distance puts the guide on the other side.

:::tip
Drawing tools snap to guides, and to the points where guides cross, which makes them handy for placing holes and parts exactly.
:::

### Remove all guides

Deletes every guide in the drawing.

1. Click **Remove all guides**. <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> brings them back.

### Machine area

Shows or hides your machine’s cutting area, to check the material and parts fit.

1. Click **Machine area**. The cutting area appears as a dashed outline, sized from 454 Control’s machine settings, or a machine you choose.
2. It warns you if the material is larger than the machine can cut, or if shapes sit outside the area.

## View and history

Getting around the drawing, measuring, and undoing.

<Shot src={gView} alt="The View and history group: Fit stock, Undo, Redo and Measure." />

### Fit stock

Shortcut: <kbd>Home</kbd>

Zooms and moves the view so the whole material fits the window.

1. Click **Fit stock**, or press <kbd>Home</kbd>.

:::tip
Scroll to zoom in and out, and drag with the right or middle mouse button to move around.
:::

### Undo

Shortcut: <kbd>Ctrl+Z</kbd>

Takes back the last change.

1. Click **Undo**, or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>. Keep going to step further back.

### Redo

Shortcut: <kbd>Ctrl+Y</kbd>

Puts back what Undo took away.

1. Click **Redo**, or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Y</kbd>.

### Measure

Shortcut: <kbd>I</kbd>

Measures without changing anything: the distance and angle between two points, or the gap between two shapes.

1. **Between two points:** click one point, then the other.
2. **The gap between two shapes:** Shift-click one shape, then Shift-click the other.
