## What 454 Control does

- Opens G-code, checks it, and shows it in 3D before anything moves.
- Runs the job, adding the things files leave out: spindle spin-up, travelling high, tool-change handling, and a predictable ending.
- Jogging, zeroing, BitZero probing and BitSetter tool measurement.
- Recovers a stopped job from the line where cutting actually stopped.

## What 454 Design does

- 2D drawing for parts you intend to cut: lines, arcs, text, and the editing tools around them.
- Dimensions that drive the geometry, in millimetres or inches.
- Opens VCarve .crv files and DXFs, and lists the toolpaths a project contains.
- Nests parts onto your material, and holds your tool library, imported from VCarve.

## What you need

- **Chrome or Edge on a desktop** to talk to the machine. Everything else works in any browser.
- **A GRBL 1.1 controller**, which covers the Shapeoko and Nomad.
- Nothing to install, and no account. Settings live in your browser and export to a file.

:::caution
**These apps move a real machine.** Keep a hand near the e-stop, and air-cut anything new before cutting material. Read [Before you start](before-you-start.html) first.
:::

## Help inside the apps

Press <kbd>F1</kbd> in 454 Design or 454 Control, or the **?** (Design) or **Help** (Control) button in the header, and these guides open
in a window over the app. It opens at the part about what you're doing: the kind of cut in the toolpath editor,
Job setup, the tool library, setting your zero. Type in the search box to find anything else (try "tabs" or
"probe"), press <kbd>Enter</kbd> for the first result, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> to go back to your work. While it's open, keys you
press don't reach the app, so nothing on the drawing or the machine changes. **Open the docs** shows the same
page in a window of its own, to keep beside your work.

The small **ⓘ** beside a section's name, in either app, does the same for that section: rest the pointer on it for a
line about what the section is for, or click it to open the guide there.
