454 Control: reference

Every part of 454 Control’s screen, and what each control does. For your first job from start to finish, see the Control quick start. The ⓘ beside each section in the app gives a short reminder of the same.

Caution

454 Control moves a real machine. Keep a hand near the stop, wear eye and ear protection, and run anything new in the air first.

The screen

Section titled “The screen”
  • The header shows the loaded file’s name and, once connected, a red STOP button, the machine’s state (such as Idle, Run or Alarm), whether the spindle is running, and Hold and Resume. On the right: Docs, 454 Design, Settings, the sample file, and Open for a G-code file (.nc, .gcode, .tap or .ngc). You can also drop a file anywhere on the window.
  • The 3D preview draws the job: cuts as solid lines, rapids dashed. Left-drag to orbit, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom. The cube in the corner turns the view to Top, Front, Left and so on, and Fit frames the whole job.
  • The summary above the tabs gives the job’s extents in X, Y and Z, its estimated run time, how far it cuts and travels, and its tools.
  • The tabs: Machine, Code, Toolpaths and Checks.
  • The bar along the bottom: the position readout and playback.

The Machine tab

Section titled “The Machine tab”

Everything for running the machine, top to bottom: connecting, homing, jogging and zeroing, probing, the spindle, quick actions, and running a job. It’s the tab Control opens on.

Connecting

Section titled “Connecting”
  1. Switch the machine on and plug it in by USB.
  2. Press Connect to machine and pick your controller from the browser’s list. After the first time, the button says Reconnect and skips the list; Choose port… picks a different one.
  3. Control reads the controller’s settings and asks to home the machine. Say yes: homing is required before a job, because lifting clear, parking and the BitSetter all use the machine’s own coordinates.

Control talks to GRBL 1.1 controllers over USB, which covers the Shapeoko and Nomad. The web version needs Chrome or Edge on a computer, because other browsers can’t talk to USB devices; the desktop app works on its own. Everything else, including opening and checking G-code, works anywhere.

Machine

Section titled “Machine”
  • Home ($H) runs the homing cycle, finding each axis’s limit switch.
  • Unlock ($X) clears an alarm without homing. Use it with care: until you home, the machine doesn’t know where it is.
  • Reset stops everything at once, as the controller’s reset does.
  • The line underneath shows the machine position, in the machine’s own coordinates.

Jog and zero

Section titled “Jog and zero”

Open jog panel moves the machine by hand and sets your work zero: where the job’s X0, Y0 and Z0 are on the material.

  • Move sets how far each press goes: 0.025, 0.1, 1 or 10 mm. In Fast, the machine moves while you hold a button or key, at the Fast XY and Fast Z speeds, and stops the moment you let go.
  • The arrow buttons move X and Y, including diagonally; Z+ and Z− move Z. On the keyboard, while the panel is open: ← → for X, ↑ ↓ for Y, PgUp PgDn for Z.
  • Rapid position moves quickly to one of nine points around the table: the corners, the middles of the edges, and the centre. It needs a homed machine.
  • Work zero: Zero X, Zero Y, Zero Z or Zero all makes the current position zero on those axes. The zero is stored in the controller, so it survives homing and restarting Control.
  • Go to XY zero raises Z first, then moves over the job’s X0 Y0. Z +6 mm lifts the tool 6 mm from where it is, as in Carbide Motion. Probe… opens the BitZero panel.

Probe

Section titled “Probe”

BitZero Z zero… sets Z zero with the BitZero touch plate, which puts Z0 at the top of your material.

  1. Set the plate’s thickness in Settings → Accessories, measured with calipers.
  2. Put the plate on the material and jog the tool over it, 2 to 10 mm above.
  3. Touch the pin to the plate to check the circuit, then probe. Control finds the plate quickly, backs off, and measures again slowly for accuracy.
  4. Check the result with a piece of paper under the tool before cutting.

Spindle

Section titled “Spindle”

Type a speed and press Start (M3), or Spindle off. With a VFD, the speed shown is what was asked for, not a measurement. Starting by hand here doesn’t wait for the spindle to reach speed, so give it a few seconds before cutting. Jobs are different: Control adds a spin-up wait after every spindle start automatically, set in Settings → Accessories.

Quick actions

Section titled “Quick actions”

Saved G-code for things you do often, like Raise Z to top or Spindle warm-up. + Add makes a new one, and the pencil edits one. Lines are sent one at a time, each waiting for the controller to confirm the last, and any error, alarm or reset stops the action at once. They’re saved with your machine profile.

Running a job

Section titled “Running a job”
  1. Load the file, check its preview and the Checks tab, and set your work zero.
  2. Press Run loaded file. If the job changes tools, Control first asks how each change should work (see below).
  3. Read the summary of what will happen, then press Start job.
  4. While it runs, Hold pauses, Resume continues, and End job stops it: it lifts clear before stopping the spindle.

Tool changes. For a job with more than one tool, Control needs a way to set each new tool’s height. With a BitSetter set up, it measures each tool automatically. Without one, it asks what you’d like to do:

At each change, the machine lifts, moves to the tool-change position, stops the spindle and asks for the new tool; with Re-zero at each change, it then asks you to set Z zero again, with the BitZero or by jogging.

Recover restarts a stopped job from a line, for example after a broken bit: type the line in from line, or scrub the preview to the spot. Control rebuilds the job’s state up to that line, moves back carefully, and lets you back out at any point.

Console

Section titled “Console”

The raw conversation with the controller: everything Control sends, and everything it says back. You can type commands here for anything the app doesn’t cover.

Caution

Commands typed in the console are sent exactly as written, with no checks and no lifting or spindle handling. Treat it as typing straight into the controller.

The Code tab

Section titled “The Code tab”

The job’s G-code, line by line. Click a line to move the preview there. With Follow playback on, the list scrolls to keep the current line in view as the preview plays. Jump to toolpath… goes to the start of any toolpath named in the file.

The Toolpaths tab

Section titled “The Toolpaths tab”

Each toolpath in the file, with its tool, estimated time and share of the job, deepest cut and cutting levels, feeds, spindle speed, the area it covers, and its lines. Click one to show just that toolpath in the preview. If a file doesn’t name its toolpaths, they’re grouped by tool change instead.

The Checks tab

Section titled “The Checks tab”

Anything in the file worth knowing before you cut: errors (red) that the machine would refuse or that would make the job go wrong, warnings (orange), and notes (blue) like tool changes. Click one to go to its line. A file with nothing to report says so.

The position and playback bar

Section titled “The position and playback bar”
  • X, Y, Z show the work position: where the tool is relative to your work zero. While connected, that’s the machine’s live position; otherwise, the preview’s.
  • line and tool show where the preview is in the file.
  • The playback buttons go to the start, step back a line, play or pause, step forward a line, and go to the end. The menu sets the speed, from 0.5× to 100×, and the slider scrubs through the job. The time on the right is the preview’s position in the estimated run time.

Playback only moves the preview, never the machine.

Settings

Section titled “Settings”

The gear in the header. Settings are saved as you change them, in your machine profile.

  • Machine: what to show in the preview; the work area (from the controller, or a machine you pick), where the job’s zero sits on the material, and whether to flag moves outside the work area; the stock thickness, to flag cuts deeper than the material; the rapid rate used for time estimates; and the controller config, the controller’s own settings as read, with Check controller settings to look them over for anything that would cause trouble.
  • Accessories: the BitSetter (jog over it and Capture position; Test touches off once to check it, changing nothing), the BitZero (v1 or v2, and the plate thickness), and the spindle (trim router or VFD, and the spin-up wait after each start: about 2.5 s suits a router, 7 s a VFD; the default is 7).
  • Job: Start & stop high, which keeps the bit above clamps and stock whenever it’s travelling (leave it on); the traverse height; how far to lift at tool changes and the end; and where to change tools and where to park at the end of a job.
  • Profile: the theme (dark or light) and accent colour, and exporting or importing your machine profile, including a Carbide Motion shapeoko.json.
