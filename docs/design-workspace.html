454 Design: workspace

Everything in 454 Design around the drawing tools themselves: setting up the job, layers, your tools, the Toolpaths panel, checking the drawing, and Settings. For the drawing tools, see Design tools; for every toolpath type and setting, the CAM reference.

The screen

Section titled “The screen”
  • The header: Docs, the Tool library, Job setup, Settings, and 454 Control.
  • On the left: the Tools tab holds the tool groups, and the Layers tab the layers.
  • In the middle: the drawing, on the material. Scroll to zoom, drag with the right or middle mouse button to move around, and press Home to fit the material in the window.
  • On the right: the Toolpaths panel. » collapses it to a narrow strip; click the strip to open it again.
  • Along the bottom: the status bar.

Job setup

Section titled “Job setup”

The material you’re cutting, and where the machine’s zero will be. Everything else is measured from these, so it’s worth setting first. Open it from the header, or Settings → Job setup….

  • Width, Height and units. The units apply everywhere: the material, dimensions, typed values, the measure tool and the readout. A unit typed after a number always wins, so 25mm works in an inch drawing.
  • XY zero: where X0 Y0 is on the material: one of the four corners, or the centre. Match it to where you’ll set X and Y zero on the machine in 454 Control. Changing it keeps your parts where they are on the material.
  • Thickness and Z zero: the top of the material, or the spoilboard under it. The thickness is used whenever something cuts right through, so measure it rather than trusting the label. Z zero must match where you zero the tool in 454 Control.
  • Machine: the machine’s cutting area (from 454 Control, a Shapeoko or Nomad size, or your own), which corner the material sits against, and how far in. It’s used to draw the cutting area (Guides → Machine area), so you can see how the material and parts sit on the bed.

Layers

Section titled “Layers”

The Layers tab, on the left. Layers keep a drawing organised, and let you hide or lock parts of it while you work on the rest.

  • The active layer is outlined: new shapes go on it. Click a layer to make it active.
  • The eye shows or hides a layer. Hidden layers are drawn struck through, and their shapes can’t be selected.
  • The lock stops a layer’s shapes being selected or changed, while still showing them.
  • The number is how many shapes the layer holds. Double-click a name to rename it.
  • With shapes selected, a layer offers to move them onto it.
  • The bin deletes a layer; the last one can’t be deleted. + Layer adds one.

A multi-sheet VCarve project brings each sheet in as its own layer; see the CAM reference for how sheets work.

The tool library

Section titled “The tool library”

Your bits, their tool numbers, and their feeds and speeds for each material. Open it from the header. It’s shared with 454 Control.

  • Import VCarve library… brings in a VCarve tool database (.vtdb), with its folders, tool numbers, and feeds and speeds for each machine and material. Or build your own: + Tool adds a bit, and + Material a material.
  • Machine and Material, at the top, choose which feeds and speeds a new toolpath takes from a tool.
  • Select a tool to see its geometry, its tool number on each machine, and its feeds and speeds for each material. Edit, Duplicate or Delete it there.
  • Search tools finds a bit by name. Tools you’ve used lately gather in a Recently used folder at the top.

The Toolpaths panel

Section titled “The Toolpaths panel”

Where a drawing becomes a job. Toolpaths are in beta: check every job in 454 Control’s preview, and run it in the air, before cutting material.

  1. Select the shapes to cut, then press + (New toolpath) at the top of the panel.
  2. In the editor, choose what kind of cut it is, the tool and the depth, and check the rest. A preview appears on the drawing as you go.
  3. Press Create. The toolpath gets a card in the panel.
  4. When they’re all made, press Save G-code… for the file to run in 454 Control, and Job sheet… for a printable page to keep at the machine.

The editor holds everything about one toolpath: its shapes, the kind of cut, the tool (its diameter comes from the tool), the depth or through, the depth per pass, and the settings that kind of cut uses, such as the stepover and clearing pattern for a pocket, the direction, and a finishing pass. Nothing is guessed: Create stays disabled until you’ve chosen a tool and a depth.

Each card shows the toolpath’s number in the cutting order, its tool and feed, and a summary of its depth and details. On each card:

  • The tick box includes it in the G-code, or leaves it out. The eye shows or hides its preview.
  • The arrows move it earlier or later in the cutting order; you can also drag a card by its handle.
  • Recalculate (↻) rebuilds it from its shapes and the material as they are now. It’s highlighted when the toolpath is out of date. Recalculate all, at the top of the panel, does every toolpath.
  • The pencil opens it in the editor; the bin deletes it.
  • Right-click a card for the same actions in a menu.

At the top of the panel: Check (below), Recalculate all, + for a new toolpath, and » to collapse the panel. A VCarve project with toolpaths offers to make them editable here.

Checking the drawing

Section titled “Checking the drawing”

Check, at the top of the Toolpaths panel, looks the drawing over before you cut: open outlines, stray pieces, shapes drawn twice, outlines that cross themselves, and shapes no toolpath cuts.

The shapes concerned are selected on the drawing, so you can see them. Fix what can be fixed does what it safely can, such as deleting a shape drawn twice, and says what’s left for you. A drawing with nothing to report says so.

Settings

Section titled “Settings”

The gear in the header.

  • Job setup… opens Job setup.
  • Theme and Accent: dark or light, and the accent colour. They’re shared with 454 Control, so both apps look the same.
  • Display colours for the drawing’s vectors, toolpath previews, construction lines and dimensions, and whether to show the dimensions.

The status bar

Section titled “The status bar”
  • X and Y on the left: where the pointer is on the drawing.
  • The middle says what the current tool wants next, and what you can type.
  • The bulb on the right shows recent messages: everything Design has told you, in case one disappeared before you read it.
